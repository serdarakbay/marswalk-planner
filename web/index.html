    Plan a walk on Mars before the first step.

    Martian Map plans a safe astronaut walk in Gale crater, along the layers where Curiosity crossed from clay-bearing into sulfate-bearing rock: a record of Mars drying out. Map, route, oxygen, radiation and a visor HUD, on one page.

    Launch planner Watch the storm

    Plate 01

    The Walk

    Three stops, from the clay-bearing unit into the sulfate-bearing unit. Each one asks a different question about how long water stayed here.

    Clay-bearingTransitionSulfate-bearing

      Why a human?

      Curiosity waits for a new plan from Earth before each choice, usually once per sol. A geologist on foot can scan a whole outcrop, follow one layer along the slope, see where the rock type changes and take several samples on both sides of that change in a single walk.

      Plate 02

      Keeping the crew safe

      Every layer on the planner map answers one question: can the crew get there, can they get back, can they talk to the habitat, and where do they hide.

      Plate 03

      The Storm

      A real solar storm reached Mars and Curiosity's radiation detector, RAD, measured it. We replay that day on the walk: the HUD raises a danger alert and the map flies to the nearest shelter.

      Plate 04

      The Visor

      Press H in the planner and the page becomes a helmet visor. A simulation clock walks the astronaut along the route, on the map and on the elevation profile.

      Panels

      • Next stop distance, time and what to look at
      • Life support oxygen left and dose so far
      • Sun elevation and direction
      • Earth link one-way signal delay and Earth above the horizon

      Alerts

      • O2 low
      • Dose over plan
      • Steep ground
      • Radio shadow
      • Shelter far

      Every number has a source

      Each value in the models is tagged: sourced from a published reference, assumed by the team, or derived from the others. We show the assumptions instead of hiding them.
